What is it

What it means to stake BNB

To stake BNB is to delegate your coins to a validator that helps secure BNB Smart Chain. Native staking now runs on BNB Smart Chain itself, so you delegate from the same wallet you already use for the network.

What you receive

When you delegate, you receive validator-specific stake credit tokens that represent your share of that validator's pool. Rewards accrue to the stake, so the value behind your share can grow over time. Rewards are not guaranteed and depend on the validator and the network.

Who it is for

Holders who plan to keep BNB for a while and are comfortable with an unbonding wait before they can move it again.

Native vs liquid

Native delegation vs liquid staking

Both ways let BNB earn staking rewards. They differ in what you hold afterwards and who you rely on.

Native delegation vs liquid staking
Native delegationLiquid staking
Who you delegate toA validator you chooseA liquid staking protocol that spreads stake
What you holdValidator-specific stake credit tokensA protocol's liquid staking token
ExitUndelegate, then wait about 7 days to claimDepends on the protocol; often a market sale or its own queue
Main risksValidator slashing, commissionSmart contract risk, token price drift, protocol fees

Which fits

Native delegation keeps you close to the chain and one validator. Liquid staking alternatives exist if you want a token you can trade or use elsewhere, with different trust assumptions.

How to stake

How to stake BNB

Before you start

Use a wallet connected to BNB Smart Chain and keep a little extra BNB aside for gas. Review the validator's commission before you commit.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and make sure it is on BNB Smart Chain.
  2. Choose a validator and review its commission.
  3. Enter the amount of BNB to delegate.
  4. Sign the delegation transaction in your wallet; your stake credit tokens appear once it confirms.
  5. To exit later, undelegate, wait out the unbonding period of about 7 days, then claim your BNB.

Details of the staking system are in the BNB Chain docs.

Fees & gas

What staking BNB costs

Gas

Delegating, undelegating and claiming are each on-chain transactions, and gas is paid in BNB. Keep some BNB unstaked in the wallet so you can always sign.

Validator commission

Validators charge a commission on the rewards earned. It is set by each validator and can differ between them, so compare before you delegate. This interface shows it before you sign.

Unbonding is a time cost

Undelegated BNB is locked for an unbonding period of about 7 days. During that time it is not usable and does not move, so plan for it.

Security

Staking safely

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. This interface never holds your BNB or your keys.

Risks to understand

  • Slashing: if a validator misbehaves, penalties can affect the delegators of that validator, not only the operator.
  • Lock-up: undelegating starts an unbonding period of about 7 days before you can claim.
  • Smart contract risk: staking contracts and liquid staking alternatives can contain bugs, so risk is never zero.
  • Check the address and network in your wallet prompt before every signature.
FAQ

Stake BNB FAQ

How do I stake BNB?

Connect a wallet on BNB Smart Chain, choose a validator, enter an amount and sign the delegation. You receive validator-specific stake credit tokens that represent your share.

Do I get rewards for staking BNB?

Rewards accrue to the stake while it is delegated, after the validator takes its commission. Amounts vary with the validator and the network, and none are guaranteed.

How long does it take to unstake BNB?

Undelegating starts an unbonding period of about 7 days. After it ends you can claim your BNB back to your wallet.

What are stake credit tokens?

They are tokens you receive when you delegate, and they represent your share of a specific validator's stake. They are tied to that validator, so they are not the same as a generic liquid staking token.

Can I lose BNB by staking?

Yes, in some cases. If a validator misbehaves, slashing can affect delegators too, so check the validator before you delegate. Smart contract risk also applies.

Which fees apply when I stake BNB?

You pay gas in BNB for each transaction, and the validator charges a commission on rewards. This interface does not hold your funds.
